# Taper to Zero

A small personal PWA for tracking a 10-day cannabis taper (Jul 12–22, 2026), then counting days at zero. Installable on iPhone via Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen.

## How it works

- **Single page, no build step.** All app logic lives in `index.html`; `sw.js` + `manifest.webmanifest` make it an installable, offline-capable PWA. Type is the system's own (SF Pro / SF Pro Rounded via `ui-rounded`, SF Mono for clock stamps) plus a self-hosted Fraunces for the wordmark; icons are one inline SVG sprite.
- **Data stays on-device.** Everything is stored in `localStorage` under the `ttz2` key — nothing leaves the phone. A backup can be copied/imported from the "Backup & data" section in the app.
- **Days end at 4am**, so a 1am session counts toward the evening it belongs to.

## Features

- **Pills** — a dose log on its own tab, beside the taper and never feeding it, and all of it is pressing. The board is a grid of tiles, one per pill: press one and a dose is logged at this minute, at the mg that pill is at. Nothing is typed and no time is picked. The main pills are built in (`PILL_MAIN`) with the strengths each is made in. Every tile carries an mg chip; pressing it opens a sheet of those strengths as buttons, and pressing one sets it — two presses to step a dose down. "Other amount" on the sheet is a ruler to drag, with − and + for a single step, for a half tablet or a pill the app has no strengths for. Each dose keeps the mg it was logged at, so changing a pill's mg never rewrites what was taken before. Supplements are smaller buttons under the tiles, pressed off a built-in list (`PILL_SUPPS`) the first time and kept on the row after that. Under the board, **what's been taken** is a picture before it is a sentence: a week of days across the top (press one to read that day, ‹ for the week before), and under it one row per pill taken that day — a capsule per dose with its mg on it, the day's total in large figures beside the name, and the week's totals as seven bars off one baseline with the chosen day lit, so a dose coming down shows as the bars do. Press a row for its times and the gap between each; press a time to walk it back with −5m / −15m / −1h (or +5m, never past now), to put right the mg it was logged at, or to remove it; every log, change and removal has an Undo. "Your pills" takes a pill off the board (its history stays, and one press brings it back) and is the only place with a keyboard: a pill added by name, and an optional minimum time between doses taken from the label or a prescriber. Only a pill with a spacing you set shows "Clear at 2:05 PM", and a press inside that spacing asks once first (never blocks). The app offers the strengths a pill comes in; which one you are at, and any spacing, is only ever yours to set. Doses live in the same record and backups as everything else
- Daily hit tally against a stepping-down cap. Today leads with **one ring**: on a taper day it fills from your last hit toward the moment the next window opens (green and full once the gap has been kept), with the time since the last hit in the middle and one line under it — the next window, "Window open", or "done for today". After zero it fills toward the next marker day (30, 60, 90…) with the day number in the middle. Per-hit clock times, yesterday and the longest gap live on the cards below it, where they can be read. Below it, the count as pips grouped by sitting (the next sitting's allowance outlined), then hold-to-log
- Per-hit timestamps drive the session rules ("max 3 hits/session · 3h between") — the today card shows when the next session opens
- Timestamps are on the surface, not buried: every day timeline stamps each sitting with its clock time, and a "Yesterday" card on the Today screen spells out the prior day's sittings and the gaps between them
- The longest gap is the headline stat — lit across the day's timeline, badged in the gap list, and compared against the previous logged day's longest everywhere a day appears (the Yesterday card also tracks the stretch running right now against yesterday's best)
- Working 20-minute urge delay timer (survives app close/reopen)
- Self-adjusting schedule: an over-cap day automatically holds its phase one extra day, pushing all later steps and the zero date back in real time (the banner lets you decline and keep the original dates); a failed step-down defers — the day rides its old cap and the drop retries tomorrow; every two hard days at one cap ease the next step by an extra base day; and zero eve offers one (only one) planned extra evening-only day while the stash still exists
- The Plan tab is four panes behind a sticky segmented control — **Today** (the day's ceiling and windows), **Ladder** (the staircase, the day strip, and a collapsed card per rung), **Change plan** (the schedule card: every move, every control, the record) and **After zero**, which appears only once there is an after. It was one accordion holding all of it, ~13k characters navigable only by scrolling; each pane is now under 5k. A live decision puts a pulsing dot on "Change plan" and opening the tab lands you there, because a pending choice is why you opened the tab even when you don't know the choice exists
- "The schedule, and why" writes that derivation out — a live decision leads it and a long record folds in behind, since a choice the app is asking for shouldn't sit under a scrolling log of the choices already made: every day that held a step, every step-down that deferred, every hold that was declined, every day added by hand — each one reversible from that card, and a manual "this step needs another day" for a rough day that still landed under its cap. Correcting a day's count also drops any stale decline on it, so an answer given once can never silently freeze the descent
- The rebuilt tail fills in the bottom of the ladder: **7, 6 and 4** join it, so below the daytime cut it walks 8 → 7 → 6 → 5 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 → 0 and no step asks for more than a single hit at a time. The fine ladder read 8 → 5 → 2–3 there — a 37% drop then a 40% one, in the stretch where every attempt on this record actually came apart. They belong to the rebuilt tail only; a first descent walking its ladder fine doesn't need nine rungs to get from 8 to zero
- **Rebuilding is not once-only, and doesn't wait for you to fail.** The re-entries are a dated list rather than a single slot, so the move can be taken as often as the ladder needs it — each one supersedes the tail before it, and every rung already walked keeps its dates and its logged days. It also has a manual door: the automatic offer fires on evidence (budget spent, days landing a whole rung above the cap), which is the right trigger for a plan that has already broken and the wrong one for someone who can see the next step coming and knows they can't hold it. "Is this ceiling too low?" is always there on a restarted descent, with every ceiling between today's and the one the descent started from
- **Hours between sittings** is a ceiling you set, from the Change plan pane. The widening gap is what the taper actually enforces — the cap says how much, the gap says the day can't be one long sitting — but a rung can ask for more hours than a person's day has shape for, and a rule broken daily stops being a rule. The ceiling only ever *narrows*: a rung already inside it keeps its own gap. When it bites, the day's windows re-space inside the hours that rung already used (same first and last window, more sittings between them, never more than the cap can fill), the note regenerates with the new arithmetic, and any advice line asserting the old gap length is dropped rather than left arguing with the number above it
- Three paces for the descent, chosen from "The schedule, and why" and reversible while you're still on the step you switched on: the **standard** plan (each ceiling two days); **smaller cuts** (the finer ladder — each remaining step split in two, so the drops are gentler but zero doesn't move); or **the gentle path** (each remaining ceiling held about a week, stretching the descent to ~3–4 weeks and moving zero later — the pace the withdrawal literature leans toward for long-term daily use, cited in the guide). Smaller cuts spend nothing against the date; only the gentle path buys time
- And a fourth move for a plan that has already broken: when the extension budget is spent and the days are still landing whole levels above the cap, the schedule card offers to **rebuild the descent** — re-enter the ladder at the ceiling the log shows holding (median of the last three full days, snapped up to a step) and take the rest as half-cuts at two days each, with a refilled budget and every pre-rebuild day kept exactly as logged. Offered once, applied on a dated tap, undoable the same day
- The clean clock keeps running after zero and ticks once a second: the hero ring switches from "time to the next session" to time since the last logged hit — exact to the second, days and hours leading with the minutes and seconds beneath — with the withdrawal phase named beside it and, under that, what the clock has bought so far (hits not taken against the ~30/day baseline, money saved if a cost per hit is set, urges outlasted). The day counter beside it stays a calendar count, because the markers and the ladder are counted in days and mean days, but it never stands alone: "day 2" always carries the exact elapsed next to it, so a day-and-three-quarters can't read as two. Those phases are read off that clock rather than off the day counter, because the literature's windows — onset 24–48h, peak days 2–6, fading across weeks 2–3, sleep settling last — are all measured from the last dose, and a calendar day count can sit most of a day away from it
- Post-zero slip logging with slip-resilient counters (current run, total clean days, best run — a slip never wipes the total)
- Check-in (sleep / mood / cravings, 1–5 — dots count what there was, so loud cravings are the high number) from the final taper phase onward, with trend chart and insights — nightly through zero day 21, weekly after, and back to nightly for a week after any slip
- The long tail: marker days at 30/60/90/180/365 with planned rewards funded by the money-saved tracker, a stale-backup nudge, and a one-sentence "why" that surfaces during the urge timer and on rough days
- Flexible in both directions: a day already lived at the next cap can end its step a day early, pulling zero closer
- And a sixth move for the opposite failure — a taper that ended before its schedule did. When the log holds a complete day with nothing on it and today is at zero too, the app offers to **finish early**: zero moves to the day after the last logged hit, the capped days still on the calendar close unwalked, and the counters re-date to the day withdrawal actually started (it is timed from the last dose either way). Reversible on the day it's taken; the zero-eve ritual stays open until it's done, since finishing early skips the evening it used to live on
- And a seventh move for the case every other one assumes away — a quit that came apart. The zero era used to be a one-way door: its undos expire the day they're offered, and every other move needs a capped rung still ahead of you, so a return had no path back at all. **Start again** closes the first quit's zero era as an interlude, re-enters the ladder and walks a fresh descent in half-steps with a refilled budget. The log recommends a ceiling; it doesn't pick one — every rung at or above today's own count is offered with the date zero would land on if you started there, floored there because a rung the day has already passed is a day born over its own cap. Nothing is erased — the whole first descent and the clean run it reached stay exactly as logged, and the new ladder starts lower *because* of them. Reachable from the schedule card the whole time a quit is running; leads the Today screen only once the log shows a pattern rather than one night
- And an eighth move for the descent the seventh one started. Every other move is written once and spent once — which meant they were all spent on the *first* descent, and a restarted ladder inherited none of them: one shape, fixed on the day of the relapse, with the ceiling stepping down underneath days that weren't moving and nothing left but "+1 day". So **rebuild this descent** is the rebase, re-armed one descent later and offered on the same evidence: budget spent, and the log landing a whole rung above today's ceiling. Re-enter where the recent days actually read, and every logged day keeps the ceiling it was lived under. Two things the original rebuild doesn't do — the **pace is chosen** (two, three or four days a rung, each showing the date it lands zero on, because two days is what the restart was already walking), and the **landing rungs come with it**, so a ladder built after a failed quit doesn't end on the same 2–3 → 0 cliff that broke it. Offered once, applied on a dated tap, undoable the same day. It leads the Today screen the way the fifth, sixth and seventh moves do, and picking a pace is a two-step: each option shows the whole ladder, its length and the date it lands zero on, and a single button names what it is about to do before you press it
- "The story" capstone card in the zero era — the whole taper in numbers, shareable
- Optional money-saved tracking: give it what a normal week used to cost and it derives the rest, since nobody can price a single hit but everyone knows the weekly number. Feeds the hero's running total and the reward ladder
- Every chart is tap-to-expand into a full-screen, fully labeled version — hour histogram, day punchcard, pace, avoided, day-vs-cap, nightly check-ins, and session gaps; swipe sideways (or tap the dots) to page between them, and lines draw themselves in
- Rotate the phone for the cockpit: a full-bleed six-tile live dashboard (today's count with yesterday-over-today clock tape, next-window countdown, taper score, urge timer, the week, zero day) — every tile taps through to its detail page
- Tap any history row to correct its count
- Copy/import JSON backup, share-sheet backup export, and a backup step in the zero-eve ritual — and every destructive restore can be undone, since the copy it replaced is stashed and now has a button to bring it back

## Design

The "Afterglow" design (Sep 2026): a luminous, dark-first health-app look with a separately tuned light theme. Colour is data, never decoration — the accent is **ember** through the taper and **jade** once zero is reached (it marks time: the gap, the hold control, today); **honey** means *at cap* and nothing else; **clay** means over cap or a slip; **iris** marks anything the schedule engine moved (always with an undo). A status colour never appears without its word. The ambient glow behind the page cools from ember toward jade as the descent progresses. Motion is spring-based and gated so it plays on first sight or real change, never on every re-render; exactly one element breathes per screen; `prefers-reduced-motion` keeps every value visible and keeps the hold-to-log fill (it is the friction, not decoration).

The tab bar is **Today · Pills · + · Journey · Plan**; More (the guide, the rules, your why, backup) is the ⋯ button in the header, beside the theme toggle.

A declutter pass (Sep 29, 2026) took out what restated something already on screen: the move offers on Today lead as one line each and open in place (the full offer is unchanged, and still on Change plan); the schedule banner is one sentence and a date; the hero dropped its score chip and the "N left" count ("1 of 3" and the pips already say it); the Yesterday card lost its 32-hour tape, its bar rows and its sparkline, and carries yesterday's sitting times in the fold's summary line instead; a finished check-in folds to its three numbers; Journey's headline dropped its hour-by-hour chart, the day rows their mini-pips, and the longest-gap chart labels only today and the best day. Nothing the engine computes changed.

The stylesheet is sectioned by surface (`[F]` foundation, `[TH]` Today hero, `[TC]` Today context, `[PL]` Pills, `[J]` Journey, `[P]` Plan, `[M]` More + cockpit, `[O]` overlays, `[C]` charts); legacy token names (`--sage`, `--amber`, `--clay`, `--accent`…) are aliases onto the new tokens because the render code references them inline.

## Deploying

Served from GitHub Pages (root of `main`). When changing `index.html`, bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` so installed phones pick up the update.
